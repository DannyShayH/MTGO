Discovery [#69](https://github.com/DannyShayH/MTGO/issues/69) refines [Feature #45](https://github.com/DannyShayH/MTGO/issues/45). Interview questions Q1–Q24 and final confirmation Q25 were agreed on 12 November 2026. The team confirmed shared understanding and explicitly invoked the specification step. The [Feature specification](/MTGO/product/customer-accounts-spec/) synthesizes this discovery. This page does not claim implemented or verified behavior.

## Agreed journey

- Customers create their own accounts with email and password, reusing Medusa Customer and Auth. Social login, sign-in without a password and operator-created Customer accounts are outside this Feature.
- Visitors can browse and build a basket before signing in. Entering checkout requires sign-in or registration, preserves the basket and returns the Customer to checkout. The API must also reject signed-out Order submission.
- A Customer can keep multiple saved delivery addresses with one optional default, or enter a one-time destination without saving it. A complete destination is needed before ordering, rather than during registration.
- Danish and English can be selected before sign-in. Before sign-in, use a remembered choice, then browser preference, then Danish. After sign-in, use the account preference and keep it across devices. Checkout confirms the language recorded with the Order for its notifications. Later preference changes apply to future Orders.
- An Order remains owned by the Customer who placed it. Customer-to-Customer Order transfers are outside the MVP. Remove the starter transfer journey and prevent its API from bypassing that rule.
- Email verification is required before entering checkout. Verification preserves the basket and supports resending when a link expires; browsing remains public.
- Forgotten-password recovery belongs to this Feature. Send an expiring, single-use reset link, show the same request confirmation whether the account exists or not, and require sign-in after reset. Use the same recovery flow for password changes instead of a second password editor.
- Registration requires only email and password. Before ordering, require the Customer's name, reachable phone number, street and house number, Danish postal code and city. Apartment and floor details are optional. Use the confirmed phone for SMS and delivery contact. Ordering for a separate recipient is outside the MVP.
- Customers can edit their name, phone, saved addresses, language and login email. Changing login email requires the current password and verification of the new address. Until confirmation succeeds, the old email continues working. An expired link, unavailable email or failed update leaves the original account usable. Successful completion changes both sign-in and profile email, preserves the Customer's Orders and saved details, and sends a notification to the old address. Access to the old mailbox is not required. Self-service account deletion is deferred to later refinement.
- On sign-in to an existing account, its saved language wins over a different signed-out choice. Changing language afterward updates the account preference. A new account keeps the language chosen during registration.
- Checkout keeps its reviewed destination copy. Editing or deleting an address-book entry in another tab does not silently change checkout; selecting an updated address requires review. Deleting the default address leaves no default instead of choosing another automatically.
- Session expiry preserves the basket for sign-in recovery. Explicit sign-out clears this browser's access to the Customer's basket and private details, so the next person starts fresh. If basket transfer fails after sign-in, retain the basket and offer retry before checkout continues.
- Successful password reset or login-email change invalidates existing Customer sessions across devices and requires fresh sign-in with the updated credentials. Preserve the basket for recovery without allowing an old session to retain private access. Customer credential changes leave any Restaurant staff or MTOGO operator access unaffected.
- Verification, password-reset and email-change messages use a private test mailbox for the demonstrable MVP. Exercise the actual links, expiry, resending and delivery failures through captured messages; never expose recovery links publicly or bypass verification. Real outbound email is deferred. This explicitly extends the mocked-service boundary in [ADR-0004](/MTGO/architecture/decisions/0004-website-mvp-with-mocked-external-services/).
- Confirming a phone means the Customer reviews it at checkout; SMS ownership verification is outside the MVP. Require a valid mobile-number format with a country code, accepting international numbers for deliveries in Denmark. Format validation does not prove ownership or guarantee an answer. Explain its delivery-contact and status-message use.
- Require complete Danish delivery details without an external address-verification service: name, street and house number, four-digit postal code and city, with apartment/floor optional. Show clear errors and retain entered values. Feature #46 separately checks the Restaurant serves the postal code before payment.
- Each Order preserves the Customer name and phone reviewed at checkout, alongside its destination and language. Later account edits affect future Orders and do not silently redirect an active Order's SMS or delivery contact. Active-Order corrections belong to the separately defined operator process.
- Provide a paginated list of the Customer's own Orders, newest first, with reference, date, Restaurant, total and current status. Basic details preserve purchased items and confirmed delivery information. Include empty and failed-loading states. The later timeline Feature adds progress events. Ownership checks also apply to direct API requests and shared links.
- A basket preserved after session expiry remains recoverable only by its original Customer. If a different Customer signs in on that browser, they start fresh and receive none of the original Customer's basket or delivery details. An anonymous visitor's basket can still follow that visitor through registration or sign-in.
- Manual account recovery after losing both the password and mailbox access is outside the MVP. A Customer who still knows their password can use the agreed email-change flow without access to the old mailbox. Creating another account does not transfer the original Orders.
- Verification, password-reset and email-change messages use the account's saved language when generated, independently of historical Order language. Initial registration uses the language selected during registration. Signed-out recovery confirmations remain neutral about whether an account exists.
- Failed security-email delivery preserves the last usable account state and allows retry. Failed verification delivery keeps checkout locked; failed reset delivery leaves the existing password usable; failed new-address confirmation leaves the old login working. If an email change completes but notification to the old address fails, keep the change effective and retain that notification for retry and operator attention.

The underlying access, destination-history and language-history rules remain those in [ADR-0005](/MTGO/architecture/decisions/0005-single-restaurant-orders-and-historical-facts/), [ADR-0008](/MTGO/architecture/decisions/0008-role-boundaries-and-restaurant-activation/) and [ADR-0013](/MTGO/architecture/decisions/0013-order-timeline-and-independent-notification-delivery/).

## Refined stories

- As a visitor, I can browse and build a basket, then sign in or create my personal Customer account when entering checkout, keeping my selected food.
- As a Customer, I can manage saved delivery addresses or enter a one-time destination, so I confirm where each Order should go.
- As a Customer, I can choose Danish or English and keep that preference across devices, while each Order retains its confirmed communication language.
- As a Customer, I can access my own account details and Orders, while another Customer cannot gain ownership of my Orders through a transfer.
- As a Customer, I can verify my email, recover a forgotten password and change my login email while keeping my personal account and Orders.
- As a Customer, I can review my own Order history and purchased details without another Customer, Restaurant staff member or Delivery agent gaining access to my account history.
- As a Customer, I can review the phone and destination used for this Order, knowing later profile edits will not silently change an ongoing delivery.
- As a Customer, I can resume my preserved basket after signing back in without another person on the same browser gaining access to it.
- As a Customer, I can retry failed security-email delivery in my account language while the last usable account state remains intact.

## Agreed acceptance evidence

The later implementation must demonstrate the complete account journey in Danish and English, including keyboard operation, validation and recovery failures, cross-account isolation and rejection of revoked Customer sessions. Exercise direct API calls as well as storefront interactions, including wrong-owner IDs, attempted Order transfers and old-token refresh attempts. Verify that Customer security changes leave Restaurant staff and MTOGO operator access unaffected.

Verify Order-history pagination and detail access with synthetic accounts and Orders representing multiple owners and enough Orders to span pages. Apply the shared exercise target of 95% of requests completing within two seconds under twenty simultaneous journeys to account-history/detail reads. Record the workload, synthetic data, environment and results; this is an acceptance target, not a claim of measured performance. The full reporting-scale dataset remains with the reporting Features, and the paid-Order submission target remains with Feature #46.

Demonstrate preservation of confirmed destination, contact details and language through the later ordering integration. Failed security-email delivery, token expiry, resend and successful recovery must be observable through the private test mailbox. These acceptance examples guide later tests and do not replace the team's separate specification and work-item steps.

### Further installed-source findings

- `apps/backend/node_modules/@medusajs/medusa/dist/api/store/orders/[id]/route.js` deliberately permits Order retrieval using the Order ID without authenticating its owner. That built-in behavior conflicts with ADR-0008 and must be secured; protecting the account list alone is insufficient.
- The installed `api/store/carts/middlewares.js` completion entry does not require Customer authentication. The storefront's `placeOrder` in `src/lib/data/cart.ts` also proceeds with whatever auth headers are available. Enforce signed-in, verified Customer ownership at the submission boundary and test direct API calls.
- The installed Customer-address route in `api/store/customers/me/addresses/[address_id]/route.js` scopes reads and mutation validation to the authenticated `customer_id`. Reuse it and verify that another Customer's address ID cannot be used.
- Customer profile updates in `api/store/customers/me/route.js` run `updateCustomersWorkflow`. The installed email/password provider in `apps/backend/node_modules/@medusajs/auth-emailpass/dist/services/emailpass.js` identifies a login by its `entity_id`; its provider-update implementation changes a password, not the login identifier. Changing only `Customer.email` does not establish the required login-email change. The later specification must cover both records, duplicate-email handling, verification and failure recovery without replacing the Customer or losing Order ownership.
- `apps/backend/medusa-config.ts` requires email verification for the isolated Customer provider with a 15-minute token lifetime. The verification subscriber runs a workflow that records the actual verification link through Medusa's notification module; the disposable-only `/store/test-mailboxes` route reads captured messages for browser verification and is unavailable unless `E2E_DISPOSABLE=1`. A configured disposable recipient makes delivery failure deterministic while the Customer remains unverified and checkout stays locked; signing in again retries delivery.
- Installed `@medusajs/auth/AGENTS.md` separates Auth identities from the HTTP layer's JWT/session issuance, allows multiple actor links in `app_metadata`, and documents provider-identity uniqueness on `(entity_id, provider)`. The starter's registration action also accommodates an existing identity such as an operator's email. These are warning signs for role isolation, not proof that Customer-only credential changes work. The later specification must demonstrate that a Customer change cannot alter another actor's login, recovery, verification or sessions.
- The installed email/password provider's `update` method changes the password hash. The HTTP token-refresh route in `apps/backend/node_modules/@medusajs/medusa/dist/api/auth/token/refresh/route.js` can issue a new token from an existing authenticated request. Neither finding establishes cross-device revocation. The later specification must cover rejection of old Customer tokens, refresh attempts and sessions after reset/email change; clearing the current browser cookie is insufficient evidence.
- Installed `@medusajs/order/AGENTS.md` describes Order-owned addresses and a stable `customer_id`; `dist/models/order.js` also contains an Order email and metadata. This provides reuse candidates for historical facts, but source inspection alone does not prove that the confirmed name, phone and Order language survive later Customer/address changes. Demonstrate that behavior through the later ordering integration, keeping ownership tied to the Customer rather than a mutable email.

The [recipe compatibility review](/MTGO/architecture/restaurant-recipe-review/) remains the starting point. Keep the installed Medusa version and reuse commerce workflows. Feature #46 must receive an authenticated, verified Customer and the confirmed destination, contact phone and language; it owns the later payment and paid-Order journey.

## Optional location-based language

At the team's explicit request, [issue #83](https://github.com/DannyShayH/MTGO/issues/83) captures a separate **Could have**: suggest a preferred language from the Customer's current location, editable in settings. It does not block the baseline language journey. The location source, mapping and fallback need later refinement; an explicit setting takes precedence and existing Order language remains historical.

## Discovery confirmation

All product questions Q1–Q24 are answered. The team confirmed shared understanding in Q25 on 12 November 2026 and explicitly invoked the specification step. The agreed outcome is personal Customer accounts with verified checkout access, isolated basket recovery, editable saved details and login email, bilingual preferences and security messages, preserved Order facts, and private own-Order history. The private test mailbox exercises real security links and delivery failures within the MVP's mocked-service boundary.

Real outbound email, manual recovery after losing both password and mailbox access, SMS ownership verification, external address verification, self-service account deletion and Customer-to-Customer Order transfers remain outside this MVP. Location-based language stays in the separate Could have issue #83. Payment and Order placement remain with Feature #46; progress events remain with the later timeline Feature.

No product disagreement remains parked. The [Feature specification](/MTGO/product/customer-accounts-spec/) records the agreed contracts and testing seams. Work-item creation remains a separate step for the team to invoke. Feature #45 stays open until its business outcome and required work items are delivered and accepted.

## Implementation follow-up

The specification's behavioral contracts still need implementation design and evidence. In particular, resolve role-isolated identity changes, consistent updates to Customer and login records, duplicate-email races, interrupted/repeated confirmations, invalidation of superseded security links, and Customer-session revocation across devices. Do not claim these follow automatically from the installed APIs. Document exact validation rules, token lifetimes and resend/abuse controls in the relevant implementation work items while preserving the specified journeys.

Feature #45 owns account access, saved details, preferences and the own-Order history surface. Feature #46 consumes the authenticated, verified Customer and the reviewed destination, contact details and language, and demonstrates their preservation when placing an Order. The later timeline Feature supplies business progress events. Their integration must preserve the agreed rules without claiming those later capabilities are already delivered.

## Account language

Issue [#85](https://github.com/DannyShayH/MTGO/issues/85) adds the account language
journey. Before sign-in, the storefront chooses the remembered language, then the
browser preference, then Danish. The sign-in language selector remembers explicit
choices across visits. Registration saves its language with the Customer.

After sign-in, the saved account language takes precedence. The account page
shows a keyboard-operable Danish/English selector and save button, with translated
pending, success and failure states. A later sign-in on another browser restores
the saved preference even when that browser remembers another language. Older
accounts without a preference adopt the resolved browser language at sign-in.

The existing Customer API exposes `metadata.preferred_language` for later
security-message generation. Order language is historical and is never rewritten
by a preference change. HTTP coverage verifies supported values, invalid input,
wrong-owner and wrong-role refusals, persistence and preserved Order language;
Playwright covers browser fallbacks, remembered choices, registration, account
settings and another-device sign-in.
