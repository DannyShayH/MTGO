A customer chooses food, pays with the order, follows its progress and rates the experience after delivery. The agreed MVP uses website ordering with mocked payment and browser SSE updates, in Danish and English. Native apps, telephone ordering and scheduled orders are outside the MVP; see the [MVP boundary](/MTGO/product/mvp-and-roadmap/). The [Customer Order tracking discovery](/MTGO/product/customer-order-tracking/) records Feature #50's timeline, delivery map and recovery decisions.

## Case requirements

- Customers have personal accounts. MTOGO supports Danish and English.
- Restaurants choose their own menus. MTOGO displays their offerings.
- Customers pay with the order through an external payment supplier. A mock of that service is permitted.
- There is no additional delivery charge; delivery is included in menu prices. Delivery agents do not handle payment.
- Customers receive SMS or app updates during preparation and delivery.
- After delivery, customers are asked to rate the food, overall experience and individual delivery agent.

## Proposed journey

**Actor:** Customer. **Goal:** Receive the selected food at the delivery address and understand what happens to the order.

## Exceptions to discuss

Use these scenarios to test the journey before splitting it into implementation issues.

<ul class="review-criteria">
  <li>
    **The Customer selects another Restaurant.** Explain the one-Restaurant
    basket limit and offer an explicit choice to clear the current basket and
    start with the other Restaurant. Browsing or selecting a dish must not
    silently discard the current basket.
  </li>
  <li>
    **A dish or Restaurant changes during checkout.** If a dish is sold out, its
    price changes in either direction, or the Restaurant stops accepting new
    Orders, stop submission, show what changed and require another review before
    payment. Keep selections visible for correction; do not silently remove or
    substitute food or charge for an unavailable dish.
  </li>
  <li>
    **Payment is declined or uncertain.** A definite decline places no Order;
    keep the basket and reviewed details for another attempt and retain the
    failed-attempt history. Recheck eligibility, availability and price before
    that attempt, requesting another review only if something changed. An
    uncertain result freezes the submitted basket and price, shows a persistent
    checking state and prevents edits or another charge attempt for that
    purchase. The signed-in Customer can return to its status from checkout or
    their account using a reference that is not an Order number. They may
    explicitly start a separate basket with its own identity while the first
    purchase remains frozen. After five minutes unresolved, alert an operator
    while the Customer still sees the pending result.
  </li>
  <li>
    **Payment succeeds after a delay.** Place the Order using the details the
    Customer reviewed at submission, even if the dish or Restaurant changed
    while the payment result was uncertain. The Restaurant then accepts or
    rejects it under the existing rules; do not change its food or price.
  </li>
  <li>
    **A charge succeeds but Order confirmation fails.** Check whether Medusa
    completed the cart and recover the same Order reference without another
    charge. If completion failed after capture, use Medusa's compensation to
    refund it. Show a checking state until the outcome is confirmed; keep an
    unsuccessful refund visible as a full refund owed for operator attention.
  </li>
  <li>
    **The Restaurant rejects the Order.** Rejection or five minutes without
    acceptance cancels it with a full refund owed. A staff-provided rejection
    reason is optional, as agreed during Feature #47 discovery #139. Failed
    refunds stay unresolved. Customers may cancel before acceptance from their
    Order page, which offers Cancel only while the server allows it and then
    shows the cancellation and the full refund's outcome separately (#145). The
    Order page's food-progress timeline keeps the stages the Order really
    reached and ends in the cancellation instead of a completed path (#147).
    Operator handling is required afterward, with no automatic change-of-mind
    refund once preparation starts.
  </li>
  <li>
    **Handover cannot be completed.** Contact attempts and operator escalation
    after ten minutes at the destination are required for an unreachable
    Customer. Never report a false delivery. Customer-caused failure has no
    automatic refund; MTOGO-caused failure requires a full refund.
  </li>
  <li>
    **An SMS cannot be delivered.** The website timeline remains available.
    Notification failure does not block food preparation or delivery; unresolved
    messages stay visible to operators and retries avoid duplicates.
  </li>
</ul>

The confirmed [Restaurant Order acceptance and preparation discovery](/MTGO/product/restaurant-order-preparation/) records Feature #47's staff journey, historical kitchen details and Customer progress decisions.

## Open decisions

- [x] **Account flow:** Free browsing and sign-in before Order submission. Telephone ordering remains outside the MVP.
- [x] **Order scope:** One Restaurant location per Order, one Customer, one destination and one intended delivery. Ordered names, quantities, prices and the destination remain historical facts after later changes.
- [x] **Availability:** New Orders require an active, manually open Restaurant serving the postal-code destination and offering the selected items. Changes before payment require Customer review of the updated basket.
- [x] **Payment and acceptance:** Charge at submission and place the Order on success; Restaurant rejection or five-minute acceptance timeout cancels it with a full refund owed. Failed refunds stay visibly unresolved.
- [x] **Scheduling:** Immediate delivery in the MVP; scheduled orders are outside it.
- [x] **Feedback:** One voluntary, uneditable submission by the ordering Customer within seven days of delivery, with separate 1–5 food, experience and agent ratings.
- [x] **Updates:** The website timeline and mocked SMS cover placement, acceptance, preparation, readiness, pickup, delivery, cancellation and the Feedback invitation, in the Order language. Failed SMS does not block fulfilment and stays visible for operator attention.

Danish and English category and dish names are required; descriptions need both languages when supplied. Status messages use the language recorded with the Order. Complaint management is a future extension, outside the current project scope.

## Discovery #130: paid ordering

### Refined Customer stories

- As a Customer, I can review one Restaurant's food, delivery and contact details, Order language and final DKK price before paying, so I know what I will buy.
- As a Customer, I can see whether my payment failed, is being checked or placed one paid Order, so a retry or lost response does not charge me twice.

### Medusa fit and Feature boundaries

Reuse the existing Medusa storefront cart and checkout components, JS SDK,
commerce modules and `completeCartWorkflow` as the default completion path. Use
one mock payment option; deterministic test or demo setup selects success,
decline, timeout or late resolution without Customer-facing test controls. Gift
cards, promotions and a separate delivery charge are outside the MVP. Hide
their storefront controls and prevent direct API calls from changing the
reviewed amount through those unsupported paths.

The current storefront presents a test-card form for the built-in manual
payment provider. It accepts only the displayed test card, a non-expired date
and a three-digit code, all checked in the browser. Card details are not sent
to Medusa or saved. This is a checkout demonstration, not card processing or a
server-side payment authorization; the existing API can still be called
directly. A successful simulated payment moves no money.

For the current MVP demo, the mock resolves immediately. Issues #134 and #135
were closed as not planned following the decision to defer uncertain and delayed
supplier outcomes. The earlier specification above retains those scenarios as
historical context; they are not implemented by the test-card form. Safe retries
and recovery after a lost application response remain part of issue #133.
Use “Udfyld testkort” / “Fill in test card” to supply valid demo details. An
expired date, such as `12/06`, keeps the Customer on payment with an expiry
message and focus on that field.

Checkout address labels, saved-address choices and country names follow the
Customer's Danish or English language. Validation appears after submission,
beside invalid fields and in the existing Medusa toast. Address errors from the
server remain visible inline. After the address is saved, checkout selects the
available zero-price MTOGO delivery option and advances to payment. If that
option cannot be found, the delivery step remains available with a clear failure
state; its continue action saves the single option without a separate selection
click. The open step follows the cart: a `step` in the address opens only a
step the cart has reached, so a link without one, or to a later step, opens the
first unfinished step instead of leaving every step closed. A new cart's
address holds only the country, so the address step counts as done only once a
street and an email are saved. Finished steps show a summary without empty
parts and the same teal **Rediger** / **Edit** button with a pencil, named for
its step. The delivery step shows MTOGO's one option, door delivery, as a
selected card with the destination address and "Inkluderet" / "Included".
**Betal og bestil** / **Pay and place Order** carries a lock and the total; while
the payment goes through it keeps its colour and width and cross-fades to
"Gennemfører betaling …" / "Processing payment …". Order details show the recorded dish thumbnail (or an image placeholder),
quantity, unit price and line total using the Order's recorded currency.
For existing Orders without a Medusa thumbnail, the storefront reads the linked
Restaurant's current menu photo by variant. This is a current presentation
image, not a historical photo snapshot; unavailable or removed menu photos keep
the placeholder. Recorded names, quantities and prices are preserved. Account
overview and paginated history share the same responsive Order row and expose
one keyboard-accessible detail link per Order.
Photo lookup uses the Restaurant ID already recorded on the Order line when a
Restaurant relation is absent from the Store response. Order detail resolves the photos; overview and history use a Medusa bag icon
and total item count, so a large Order stays one compact row without fetching
menu photos for every history entry. The overview requests at most five Orders;
the full history shows ten per page and offers Medusa's built-in status filter.
Applying a filter starts at page one, and pagination retains that filter. These
are server-side pages of the Customer's own Orders, not filtering of one loaded
page or a download of all their history.

The current menu route supplies each dish's variant, revision, price and
availability for the pre-payment comparison. Existing completion middleware
protects Customer ownership and reviewed address/contact details. Add checks
for Restaurant eligibility, menu changes and payment amount in the built-in
workflow's `validate` hook, which runs under its cart lock before Order
creation. Reject changed carts for Customer review; do not mutate them inside
that hook. Refresh the payment collection before completion when the cart
changes, and verify the session amount still matches the reviewed total.

Installed Medusa 2.22.4 creates a provisional Order row before payment
authorization, then compensates on failure and returns `type: "order"` only
when completion succeeds. This internal sequence is acceptable only if an
unpaid row is never presented as a placed Order to any actor. The mock payment
option must not use `pending_authorization` for an uncertain result: Medusa
would otherwise complete the cart with an unpaid Order. The later specification
chooses a supported pending/reconciliation path and verifies rollback, late
success, amount matching and idempotency without replacing the built-in
commerce workflow unless evidence requires it.
See Medusa's [cart-completion guide](https://docs.medusajs.com/resources/storefront-development/checkout/complete-cart)
and [deferred-payment behavior](https://docs.medusajs.com/resources/commerce-modules/payment/payment-flow).

Feature #46 creates the paid Order linked to its Restaurant and shows it to the
Customer. Feature #47 adds the Restaurant staff Orders list and acceptance and
preparation actions in the partner app. Feature #49 owns the operator interface
for investigating unresolved payment and refund exceptions; #46 stores and
exposes the facts it needs.

Keep Medusa's built-in Products list visible for MTOGO operators. Add one
read-only Product-detail panel using the Admin extension system to show a
dish's Restaurant, Restaurant-owned category, Danish and English names,
sold-out state and registered photo. Reuse Medusa's layout and components;
do not build another product list or Admin menu editor. Restaurant staff
continue to change menu content and photos in the partner app. A Restaurant
is linked to its dish Products; it is not a Medusa Product category. The
starter's Shirts category and clothing Products are sample merchandise, not
Restaurants. Retire those known starter products and categories from normal
new-environment setup and safely clean up only identifiable demo merchandise
in existing environments. Update the existing browser verification fixtures
that currently depend on starter clothing.

Dish variants have inventory tracking disabled because staff mark dishes sold
out manually. Hide Inventory with Medusa's [team-default sidebar layout](https://docs.medusajs.com/user-guide/tips/layout-configurations),
without disabling the underlying commerce modules. Sidebar visibility alone
does not restrict direct URL or API access.

The later `/to-spec` chooses the exact recovery workflow, payment-attempt
storage, integration seams and verification setup. The team calls
`/to-tickets` separately after that spec.

### One Restaurant basket (#131)

A basket holds dishes of one Restaurant. The storefront menu has an "Add to basket" button on each dish that can be ordered; it is hidden for a sold-out dish and for a Restaurant that is not receiving new Orders. Feedback is a Medusa UI toast in the Customer's language.

On the menu, each orderable dish has a compact "Læg i kurv" / "Add to basket" pill on its price row. Once the dish is in the basket the pill becomes a quantity stepper (minus or trash, the count, plus) that reflects the real basket when the page loads. The stepper is the confirmation: adding a dish shows no toast and does not open the header basket over the menu. On a phone, a "Se kurv" / "View basket" bar with the item count and total stays at the bottom of the menu. The tap is answered at once and only moves forward: the pill becomes the stepper once, and the header badge and the basket bar count the dish in the same moment, before the basket is read again. If the add fails, the pill comes back, both counts go back and a toast says why. Each count stays until a basket read made after the server confirmed it, so it never jumps back and forth.

The header basket and the basket page (`/cart`) show the dishes of one Restaurant with their current menu names and photos, the Restaurant's name and one total in DKK that includes VAT and delivery. The basket page uses the same stepper as the menu and has no discount code or delivery charge, since checkout refuses both. A line records its Restaurant in `metadata.mtogo_restaurant_id` so the basket can show the menu's photos; this is display data only, and the backend decides ownership itself.

Dishes are priced in DKK only, so Denmark must be in a DKK region. The idempotent Denmark region migration script (`ensureDenmarkRegionWorkflow`) moves `dk` out of the starter's EUR region into a "Denmark" DKK region. Without it, adding a dish fails with "Variants ... do not have a price". Run `npx medusa db:migrate` in existing environments.

Rules live in the backend, so direct Store API calls cannot bypass them. Each is a stable code in the error response `code` field:

| Code                      | When                                                                                                                     | Customer sees                                                                                                                             |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `basket_other_restaurant` | A dish is added from another Restaurant. The response also has `current_restaurant_id` and `requested_restaurant_id`.    | A warning toast that stays until dismissed with the action "Clear basket and add". The basket is unchanged until the Customer chooses it. |
| `basket_not_a_dish`       | The variant is not a published Restaurant dish, such as an arbitrary Product.                                            | An error toast that the dish cannot be added.                                                                                             |
| `basket_review_required`  | `POST /store/carts/{id}/complete` finds the basket changed or ineligible. The response has `restaurant_id` and `issues`. | A message that nothing was charged, naming each dish that changed or is no longer available, or the Restaurant or amount rule.            |

Each issue is one of `dish_unavailable`, `dish_sold_out`, `dish_changed` (revision or price differs in either direction, or no snapshot was recorded; it carries the current `revision` and `price`), `restaurant_not_receiving_orders` (with `reason`, such as `restaurant_closed` or `destination_not_served`) and `amount_not_allowed`. Menu prices include VAT and delivery, so the amount must be the plain DKK item total: a promotion, discount, gift card or other credit line, a delivery charge, or a started payment for another amount or currency is refused with `amount_not_allowed`. A cart line records what the Customer saw in `metadata.mtogo_dish` (`revision` and `price`). The check runs in the `validate` hooks of `addToCartWorkflow` and `completeCartWorkflow` (`apps/backend/src/workflows/hooks/restaurant-basket.ts`), never edits the cart, and is skipped for an already completed cart. Removing every line lets the Customer start with another Restaurant.

When completion is refused, checkout reads `GET /store/carts/{id}/basket-review`, names the affected dishes and records the current revision and price of each changed dish on its line. The basket is kept: the Customer checks the new total and places the order again, which is the second explicit review. A sold-out or removed dish must be removed first.
